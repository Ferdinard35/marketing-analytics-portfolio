## The situation

> Marketing reports: "Conversion rate is up 15% this month, great work!"
> Finance reports: "Revenue per new customer is down 20%."
>
> Both are true.

**What questions do you ask before deciding whether this quarter was good or bad?**

## How to approach it

- You are the analyst in the room. Nobody has asked you for a verdict yet; they need to know what to find out.
- For each question you would ask, say what the answer would change. A question whose answer changes nothing is not worth asking.
- Write prose a manager could read in one minute, not a list of every metric you know.

## What I grade

- Whether you work out what each of the two numbers is actually measuring.
- Whether your questions are specific enough to be answered with data.
- Whether you say what would make the quarter good and what would make it bad.
- Whether it is clear, and inside the word limit.

## Your answer

Both reports can be true because they measure different things. Conversion rate says how many people came through; revenue per new customer says what each one is worth. Before judging the quarter I would ask four things.

First, what exactly converted? Conversion from what to what, on how much traffic, and is 15% relative or in points? If traffic was flat, 15% more customers each worth 20% less is about 8% less new-customer revenue (1.15 × 0.80 = 0.92).

Second, did the mix change? If growth came from a channel or campaign that converts easily and brings low spenders, the average falls even though no group got worse.

Third, was conversion bought? A sign-up bonus or fee waiver lifts conversion and cuts early revenue by design.

Fourth, is it timing? This month's customers have had less time to transact. I would compare cohorts at the same age, and their retention.

The quarter was good if the new customers are cheap enough to acquire and stay long enough that their lifetime value still beats their cost. It was bad if we paid the same, or more, for people who transact once and leave.
