# CediFlow web sign-ups: where the next ones are

**To:** Head of Growth · **Period:** 30 March to 28 June 2026 · **Source:** website analytics and ad-platform cost (synthetic data)

## The quarter

The website took 31,167 sessions and produced **2,426 sign-ups, a conversion rate of 7.8%**. Ad spend of GHS 22,218 bought 885 of those sign-ups at **GHS 25.11 each**.

The cheapest sign-ups available next quarter are ones CediFlow is already paying for and losing. Four of the five actions below need no extra media budget.

## What the data shows, and what to do

| | Finding | Action | What to expect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Mobile visitors abandon the last step.** After verifying their phone, 61% of mobile visitors finish the details form against 85% on desktop. Mobile is 77% of traffic, and 1,045 mobile sessions got that far and ended without a sign-up. | Rework the details form for mobile. Start by tracking each field, because the data shows where people leave but not why. | Closing half the gap adds about **320 sign-ups a quarter (+13%)** at no media cost. |
| 2 | **The cashback promo page does not convert.** `/promo/cashback` converts at 0.7% against 8.6% for the rest of the site, and it underperforms inside every channel that uses it. TikTok's Spark campaign sends all its traffic there: GHS 1,220 bought 12 sign-ups, GHS 102 each. | Pause Spark until the page is fixed. Meanwhile point its ads at `/students`, or move the budget to TikTok's campus campaign (GHS 9.70 per sign-up). | At half the campus campaign's rate, the same spend yields about **70 sign-ups instead of 12**. |
| 3 | **A Meta campaign is paying for visitors who cannot sign up.** 39% of META_Lookalike_Remitters clicks come from the UK and US. Sign-up needs a Ghanaian number, so 626 sessions produced 5 sign-ups. | Restrict the campaign to Ghana. | About **GHS 1,200 a quarter** stops being wasted; cost per sign-up falls from GHS 46 to about GHS 31. |
| 4 | **iOS phone verification broke for 12 days and nobody saw it.** From 18 to 29 May, 21% of iOS visitors passed the OTP step against 71% normally. About 38 sign-ups were lost. Site-wide conversion only dipped from 7.7% to 6.5%. | Alert on each funnel step by operating system: a 3-day rate against its 4-week baseline. | The alert would have fired on **day 3 instead of never**. |
| 5 | **Ashanti converts better and gets less.** Ashanti converts at 10.6% against 7.7% in Greater Accra (10.2% on a like-for-like channel mix), yet receives 15% of paid traffic against Accra's 56%. | Run a four-week test moving part of one campaign's budget from Accra to Kumasi. | A lower cost per sign-up, if the advantage holds for paid traffic. This one is a test, not a certainty. |

## One more thing worth a decision

390 sessions from abroad started the sign-up form this quarter, at the same rate as sessions in Ghana, and 380 of them ended without a sign-up. That is unmet demand from the diaspora. A waitlist on the form for non-Ghanaian numbers would measure it for almost nothing.

## How sure we are

The gaps in findings 1 to 4 are far too large to be chance (p < 0.001 in each case). The expected effects are estimates: they assume a fixed page or form would perform like the comparison shown, which only a test can confirm. Costs are last-click, which flatters retargeting and brand search. A sign-up is not yet a customer: this data ends at the form, so none of this says which channel's users go on to transact.
